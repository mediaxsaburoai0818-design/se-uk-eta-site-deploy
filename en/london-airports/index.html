Arriving and staying in the UK

London's 5 main airports

England's — and the UK's — second-largest airport is London Gatwick, with 42.8 million passengers in 2025, after Heathrow (84.5 million). Third is Manchester (32.1 million), according to the UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA). London is served by five major airports, and your choice affects your route, journey time and cost into the centre.

  3. /London's 5 main airports

Heathrow (LHR) — west London

The UK's largest airport and its most important international hub (Terminals 2–5).

Getting there: Heathrow Express about 15 min, Elizabeth line about 30 min, Underground about 50 min. More in our guide to Heathrow Airport.

Gatwick (LGW) — south London

England's and the UK's second-largest airport (after Heathrow); served by both traditional and low-cost airlines (North and South Terminals).

Getting there: Gatwick Express about 30 min, Thameslink about 30–45 min.

Stansted (STN) — north-east London

Ryanair's base, with many European connections; a single terminal that is easy to find your way around.

Getting there: Stansted Express about 50 min, coach about 60–90 min.

Luton (LTN) — north of London

A base for easyJet and Wizz Air; a single terminal, with a shuttle to the station.

Getting there: Luton Airport Express about 30 min, Thameslink about 30 min.

London City (LCY) — central London (closest)

The closest airport to the centre; popular with business travellers.

Getting there: DLR about 15–25 min to the centre.

Journey times are approximate and depend on your destination in London and the time of day.

Before you land

Whichever airport you arrive at, the same entry rules apply — see the guide Entering the UK: entry requirements and visas.
