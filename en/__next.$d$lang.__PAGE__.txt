    <div class="body">
      <span class="eyebrow">Overview</span>
      <h2>What is the UK ETA?</h2>
      <p>The UK ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation) is a digital travel permission that travellers from visa-free countries need before visiting the United Kingdom for tourism, business, short courses of study or transit. It is not a visa.</p>
      <a class="textlink" href="/en/what-is-the-eta/">Read more about the UK ETA <span class="arrow" aria-hidden="true">→</span></a>
    </div>
    <div class="visual"><img src="/home/union-jack.jpg" alt="The Union Jack flag against a blue sky" width="1200" height="840" loading="lazy"></div>
  </article>

  <article class="block" id="vem">
    <div class="body">
      <span class="eyebrow">Who needs it?</span>
      <h2>Who needs a UK ETA?</h2>
      <p>Travellers from visa-free countries, including Australia and New Zealand, need a UK ETA for short stays anywhere in the United Kingdom: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.</p>

</div><section class="band xsec"><div class="container"><span class="eyebrow">Validity and timing</span><h2>How long the ETA is valid and when to apply</h2><p class="xlead">When to apply and how long your ETA remains valid.</p><div class="xcards x2"><div class="xcard"><h3>Validity</h3><p>The ETA is valid for <strong>2 years</strong> from the date it is issued and allows multiple trips to the UK. If your passport expires within those 2 years, the ETA expires with it. In that case, renew your passport first and then apply for the ETA.</p></div><div class="xcard"><h3>When to apply</h3><p>Most applicants receive an automatic decision within <strong>a few minutes</strong> through the UK ETA app. Apply at least 3 working days before you travel, as a small number of applications need further checks. Once approved, you will receive a confirmation email. You do not usually need to show the ETA number at the border, but it is worth keeping the confirmation (a screenshot or printout).</p></div></div></div></section><section class="section xsec"><div class="container"><span class="eyebrow">Transit</span><h2>Transiting through the UK</h2><p class="xlead">Whether you need an ETA when changing planes depends on the type of transit.</p><div class="xcards x2"><div class="xcard"><span class="eyebrow">Air transit (airside)</span><h3>Usually no ETA needed</h3><p>If you do not pass through UK border control and stay in the airport transit area, you usually do not need an ETA. If you leave the transit area, for example for an overnight stay or to change to another airport, you will need an ETA.</p></div><div class="xcard"><span class="eyebrow">Land transit (landside)</span><h3>An ETA is normally required</h3><p>If you pass through UK border control, for example when travelling by Eurostar (the train through the Channel Tunnel) or by ferry, you will normally need an ETA.</p></div></div></div></section><section id="svenska" class="band xsec"><div class="container"><span class="eyebrow">For Australians and New Zealanders</span><h2>UK ETA for Australian and New Zealand citizens</h2><p class="xlead">Australian and New Zealand citizens can visit the UK without a visa for stays of up to 6 months. They must, however, hold a UK ETA before travelling. This applies to holidays, business trips, family visits and transit.</p><div class="xcards x4"><div class="xcard"><h3>Who needs an ETA</h3><p>Every Australian and New Zealand citizen travelling to the UK for a short stay, including children and infants. Each person needs their own separate application.</p></div><div class="xcard"><h3>Linked to your passport</h3><p>The ETA belongs to the passport you applied with. If you get a new passport, you must apply again, even if there is time left on the 2 years.</p></div><div class="xcard"><h3>Popular destinations</h3><p>London, Manchester, Edinburgh, Birmingham and other cities. A single ETA covers England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.</p></div><div class="xcard"><h3>When to apply</h3><p>At least 3 working days before departure. Most decisions are made automatically within a few minutes, but a small number of applications need further checks.</p></div></div></div></section><section id="faq" class="section xsec"><div class="container xnarrow"><span class="eyebrow">FAQ</span><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2><div class="xfaq"><details><summary><span>Do Australian and New Zealand citizens need a UK ETA?</span><span class="xplus" aria-hidden="true">+</span></summary><p>Yes. This applies to tourism, business, family visits and transit.</p></details><details><summary><span>How much does the UK ETA cost and how long is it valid?</span><span class="xplus" aria-hidden="true">+</span></summary><p>The UK ETA costs £20. It is valid for 2 years from the date it is issued and allows multiple trips to the UK, each of up to 6 months.</p></details><details><summary><span>What is the difference between a UK ETA and a visa?</span><span class="xplus" aria-hidden="true">+</span></summary><p>The UK ETA is a simplified digital authorisation for short stays (up to 6 months). It costs £20 and you apply online. A UK visa requires an appointment at a visa application centre, costs from £100 and allows longer stays or work.</p></details><details><summary><span>Do children need their own UK ETA?</span><span class="xplus" aria-hidden="true">+</span></summary><p>Yes. Every traveller needs their own UK ETA, whatever their age, including babies and children. A parent or guardian can apply on the child's behalf using the child's passport.</p></details><details><summary><span>Can I use my existing ETA with a new passport?</span><span class="xplus" aria-hidden="true">+</span></summary><p>No. The UK ETA is linked to the passport you applied with.
