Arriving and staying in the UK

Entering the UK: entry requirements and visas

A complete guide to the rules for entering the United Kingdom: when a UK ETA is enough, when you need a visa, how border control works and which documents to have ready.

  1. UK ETA Guide
  2. /Entering the UK: entry requirements and visas

ETA or visa? Stays of up to 6 months

For tourism, business, visiting family or transit, on stays of up to 6 months, EU citizens do not need a visa — a UK ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation) is enough. You apply online and most people get an automatic decision within minutes, but apply at least 3 working days before you travel, because some applications may need further checks.

An ETA costs £20, is valid for 2 years and allows multiple entries. Read more in What is the UK ETA?

Travelling on a passport from another country? Check whether you need an ETA or a visa with the GOV.UK tool Check if you need a UK visa.

When you need a visa

A UK visa is required for stays longer than 6 months and for long-term study, work or settlement. The most common types are:

  • Student visa — long-term study (usually 2 to 5 years depending on the course).
  • Work visa — working in the UK; the type depends on the job offer and your qualifications.
  • Family visa — to join a family member who lives in the UK.
  • Youth Mobility Scheme — for young people from selected countries (stays of up to 2 years).

Border control: eGates and manual passport checks

At UK airports, border control takes place either at automatic eGates (using a biometric passport) or at a staffed desk with a Border Force officer. eGates speed up the passport check; if there are questions about the purpose of your trip, you may be asked to speak to an officer. Who can use the automatic gates, and how to go through them step by step, is explained in the guide Passport control and eGates.

Customs comes after passport control — which foods and goods you can bring is covered in the guide Customs rules and allowances.

Documents you need

  • A passport valid for the whole of your stay (national ID cards have not been accepted since October 2021).
  • A UK ETA linked to that passport (or the relevant visa for a longer stay).
  • Supporting documents: proof of accommodation, a return ticket and funds for your stay may be checked.

Travelling with children (under 18)

Every person must have their own UK ETA, whatever their age — including babies and children. Children travelling without one or both parents may be asked to show a document confirming that the parent or guardian has consented to the trip. It is a good idea to carry a birth certificate or a written consent letter (in English). More in the guide Travelling to the UK with children.
