Arriving and staying in the UK

What can you bring into the UK? UK customs rules

The most important rule: you cannot bring meat or dairy products from the EU into the UK. Below: which foods are allowed, the exception for baby food, the alcohol and tobacco allowances, and when cash must be declared.

  2. /Entering the UK: entry requirements and visas
  3. /What can you bring into the UK? UK customs rules

Rule no. 1: no meat and no dairy products from the EU

Bringing meat and dairy products from the EU into Great Britain (England, Scotland, Wales) is prohibited. The ban covers:

  • Dairy products: cheese, milk, butter, yoghurt.
  • Meat: pork, beef, lamb and mutton, goat and game, and products made from them — including salami, sausages, ham, and sandwiches containing meat or cheese.

The rule also applies to small amounts in your hand luggage — a ham sandwich bought at the airport must be eaten or thrown away before the check.

The exception: baby food (up to 2 kg)

Infant formula, baby food and special foods for medical use are allowed up to 2 kg per person, provided they are in unopened branded packaging and do not need to be refrigerated before use.

Foods you can bring without problems

  • Bread (but not sandwiches with meat or dairy products).
  • Cakes without fresh cream, biscuits and chocolate.
  • Pasta and packet soups.
  • Fish, eggs and honey — allowed for personal use.

If you are unsure, check the specific product on GOV.UK before you travel — the rules vary by food category.

Alcohol and tobacco: duty-free allowances on arrival in the UK

CategoryDuty-free allowance
Beer42 litres
Still wine18 litres
Spirits (over 22%)4 litres, or 9 litres of drinks up to 22%
Cigarettes200 (or 50 cigars or 250 g of tobacco)
Other goods (gifts, souvenirs)up to £390 (£270 if arriving by private plane or private boat)

Anything above the allowances must be declared and the relevant charges paid. These are the UK's allowances on arrival; when you return home, your own country's rules apply.

Cash: you must declare £10,000 or more

If you are carrying £10,000 or more in cash (including the equivalent amount in other currencies), you must declare it — online before you travel or in the red channel on arrival. Failing to declare it can lead to the whole amount being seized and a fine of up to £5,000.

Penalties and how the check works

Prohibited foods can be seized at the border, and if you exceed the alcohol and tobacco allowances without declaring them, the goods can be held and penalties imposed. The safest option is to use the red channel if you have anything to declare.

Customs comes after passport control — see how passport control and the eGates work. Remember that you need a valid UK ETA to enter — you can follow your application with the ETA status check. The general entry rules are in the guide Entering the UK: entry requirements and visas, and transit through the UK has its own guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can I bring cheese or meat products from the EU to the UK?
No. Bringing meat (pork, beef, lamb and mutton, goat and game) and meat products, as well as dairy products (cheese, milk, butter, yoghurt), from the EU into Great Britain is prohibited. The products can be seized at the border.
How many cigarettes and how much alcohol can I bring into the UK duty-free?
200 cigarettes (or 50 cigars or 250 g of tobacco), 42 litres of beer, 18 litres of still wine, and 4 litres of spirits over 22% or 9 litres of alcoholic drinks up to 22%.
Do I have to declare cash when entering the UK?
Yes, if you are carrying £10,000 or more. You declare it online in advance or in the red channel on arrival. Failing to declare it can lead to the whole amount being seized and a fine of up to £5,000.
