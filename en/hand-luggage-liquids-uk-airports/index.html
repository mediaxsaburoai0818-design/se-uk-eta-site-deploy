Arriving and staying in the UK

Liquids in hand luggage and security at UK airports

At many UK airports a limit of 100 ml per container still applies to liquids in hand luggage. Some airports have started allowing larger amounts thanks to new scanners, but the rules vary — here is what usually applies and why you should check your own departure airport.

  3. /Liquids in hand luggage and security at UK airports

The 100 ml rule and the clear bag

At many UK airports, liquids in hand luggage must be in containers of no more than 100 ml each. A container that holds more than 100 ml is usually not accepted, even if it is not full.

  • The containers usually have to go in a single clear, resealable bag of no more than 1 litre.
  • The rule is usually one bag per person.
  • Gels, pastes, creams, sprays and similar products often count as liquids too.

As the details can differ between airports, it is sensible to check the current rules for your departure airport before you travel.

New CT scanners: some airports may allow more

Some UK airports have introduced new CT scanners and, with them, may allow liquids in containers of up to 2 litres. The roll-out is happening at different speeds, however, and the situation varies between airports.

Even in mid-2026 the 100 ml limit still applies at many airports. So do not assume the higher limit applies — always check the latest rules for the airport you are flying from, and ideally for your return airport too.

Exceptions: baby food, baby milk and medicine

Baby food and baby milk, as well as essential medicine, can in some cases be carried in larger amounts than the usual liquid limit. You will usually need to declare them to the staff at the security check.

The amounts and conditions can vary between airports. It can help to have a prescription or other documents to hand, and to check in advance what applies where you are travelling. If you are travelling with children, see also our guide to travelling to the UK with children.

Electronics and larger items

Larger electronic devices, such as laptops, may need to be taken out of your bag and placed separately in the tray as instructed by staff. This too can differ depending on the airport and the equipment used at the check, so follow the instructions given on the spot.

How to prepare for the security check

To make the check go smoothly, it helps to pack your liquids in the clear bag at home, to keep baby food and medicine easy to reach so you can declare them, and to check the current rules for your airport in advance.

Security comes before passport control — see how passport control and the eGates work, and what you can bring through UK customs. Remember that you need a valid UK ETA to enter — you can follow your application with the ETA status check. The general entry rules are in the guide Entering the UK: entry requirements and visas, and transit through the UK has its own guide.

Frequently asked questions

How much liquid can I take in hand luggage at a UK airport?
At many UK airports a limit of 100 ml per container often applies, with the containers in one clear, resealable bag of no more than 1 litre, one bag per person. Some airports have introduced new CT scanners and may allow up to 2 litres, but this varies between airports. Always check the current rules for your departure airport before you travel.
Can I take water through security?
Water and other drinks bought after the security check can usually be taken on board. Drinks you bring before the check are, however, usually covered by the liquid rules and may have to be thrown away if they exceed the limit. Rules can differ between airports, so check what applies where you are travelling.
Can I take baby milk and medicine through security?
Baby food, baby milk and essential medicine can in some cases be carried in larger amounts than the usual liquid limit, provided you declare them to the staff at the check. The amounts and conditions can vary, so check with your airport in advance and have any prescription or documents to hand.
